# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

This project is for people who are learning. Everyone who takes part, as a contributor or a maintainer, pledges to make it a friendly, harassment-free place for all, whatever their age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps:

- Being kind and patient, especially with beginners and with people writing in a second language
- Giving and accepting feedback gracefully
- Assuming good intent, and explaining rather than dismissing
- Owning mistakes, apologising and learning from them

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances
- Trolling, insults, put-downs, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing someone's private information, such as a physical or email address, without their permission
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional or classroom setting

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces (issues, pull requests, discussions and review comments) and wherever someone is officially representing the project.

## Reporting a problem

If you see or experience something that breaks these rules, contact the maintainer, Salman Sadik Siddiquee, privately through the contact options on this GitHub profile: <https://github.com/salsadsid>. You can also use GitHub's own "Report content" option on any comment.

Every report will be read and handled promptly and fairly, and the reporter's identity will be kept private.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and may block someone temporarily or permanently for behaviour that is threatening, offensive or harmful. The usual path is a private warning first, then a temporary block, then a permanent one, but serious harm can lead straight to a ban.
